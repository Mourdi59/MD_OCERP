## rfi
KEY: rfi.intro_more

Press **New RFI** and write the subject and question, set a priority, pick a discipline, name who holds the ball in court and an assignee, then add a response due date. You can attach the drawings in question, either ticking existing project documents or dropping a file straight into the dropzone to upload it. Each RFI opens from Open to Answered to Closed, and the row shows a ball-in-court badge plus a running days-open and overdue counter.

When an answer carries cost, expand an answered or closed RFI and press **Create Variation** to push it into the commercial trail. Attachments are pulled from Documents, the ball-in-court person comes from your team, and answered RFIs feed Correspondence as a linked thread.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Use the saved-view chips (Mine, Awaiting me, Overdue) to scan what is on your plate.
- Set a realistic due date so the overdue pill flags stalls early.
- Flag cost impact on the RFI so the Variation hand-off is one click later.

## submittals
KEY: submittals.intro_more

Press **New Submittal**, give it a title and spec section, pick the type (shop drawing, product data, sample, mock-up, test report, certificate or warranty) and a date required. The submittal starts as Draft, then you Submit it for review. A reviewer opens it and records a decision: Approved, Approved as Noted, Revise and Resubmit, or Rejected, with comments. A Revise-and-resubmit sends it back so the submitter edits and resubmits as the next revision.

The row carries a days-in-court badge and a due-date countdown so nothing stalls. Where your project has a submittal approval route configured under Governance, a multi-step sign-off runs instead of the direct review. Submittals can link to the BOQ positions they cover, and supporting files live in Documents.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Link each submittal to its BOQ positions so you can see what has approved documentation before it goes to site.
- Watch the days-in-court badge to chase a slow reviewer.

## transmittals
KEY: transmittals.intro_more

Press **New Transmittal**, write the subject, pick the purpose code (For Approval, For Information, For Construction, For Tender, For Review, For Record) and a response due date, and list the document items. You can attach controlled revisions straight from the CDE: choose a container, then add each revision so the transmittal carries the exact issued versions. The record starts as Draft, where you can still edit or delete it. Press **Issue Transmittal** to lock it and move it to Issued, after which it is read-only evidence.

The log tracks recipient acknowledgement and shows how many recipients have confirmed receipt. It comes from the CDE and feeds your Submittals and Correspondence as the dated paper trail behind them.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Attach CDE revisions rather than free text so the transmittal names the precise version issued.
- Issue only when ready, since issued transmittals lock and cannot be deleted.

## correspondence
KEY: correspondence.intro_more

Press **New Letter** and the entry opens as outgoing by default. Set the direction, pick the type (letter, email, notice or memo), enter the From and To parties, set the sent and received dates, and add notes. Save it, then expand the row to attach the source file (PDF, image, Office document) and it is stored against the entry. You can edit or delete any entry later.

Each entry can link to a related Transmittal, an RFI, and the project documents it refers to, so one communication thread stays traceable end to end if a dispute arises. Parties come from Contacts, attachments and links pull from Documents, Transmittals and RFIs. Inbound email and webhook auto-import are marked Coming Soon, so entries are logged by hand today.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Log notices the day they are sent or received so the register stays contemporaneous.
- Use the direction and type filters to pull a clean thread before a claim meeting.

## meetings
KEY: meetings.intro_more

Press **New Meeting**, choose a type (progress, design, safety, subcontractor, kickoff or closeout), set the date and time, location, chairperson and attendees, and capture the minutes. You can drop attachments onto the meeting. When the meeting has happened, mark it complete, and export a clean minutes PDF at any time. For standing meetings, set up a recurring series so the whole run is scheduled at once.

You can also import a meeting summary: upload a transcript from Teams, Google Meet, Zoom or Webex, and AI extracts the attendees, key topics, action items and decisions for you to review and edit before the meeting is created. Discussion turns into tracked action items that flow into Tasks with an owner and due date.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Import a recording transcript to skip retyping minutes and capture action items automatically.
- Review every extracted action and attendee before saving, since AI suggests and you confirm.

## moc
KEY: moc.intro_more

Press **New change request**, pick a category (engineering, scope, design, process, material, safety and more) and a risk level, name the change, describe what is changing and why, and record the headline cost and schedule impact. The request enters a controlled flow: Proposed, then Review, then Accept or Decline, then Implement. The page only offers the transitions that are legal for the current state, and each one captures a note for the audit trail.

You can add detailed impact assessment lines per area (cost, schedule, safety, quality, environment) with severity and mitigation. Approved changes carry on to Variations and Change Orders, and the row shows those links once they exist, so the commercial trail stays connected to every quality decision and dated step.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Capture the cost and schedule delta up front so reviewers approve on real numbers.
- Add a note on each transition, since the proposed, reviewed, decided and implemented dates become your audit record.

## ncr
KEY: ncr.intro_more

Press **New NCR**, pick the non-conformance type (material, workmanship, design, documentation or safety) and severity, then describe the defect, the location, and the root cause if known. The report moves through Identified, Under Review, Corrective Action, Verification and Closed. Expand a row to read the description, root cause, and the corrective and preventive actions, then press **Close NCR** when the issue is resolved.

Many NCRs are raised straight from a failed Inspection, which pre-fills the report and tags its origin (a clash or a validation failure also tags the source). When an NCR carries a cost impact you can escalate it to a Change Order so the money and the quality trail stay connected. Cross-links jump to the source Inspection, Change Orders and the Punch List.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Raise NCRs from the failed inspection rather than from scratch so they arrive pre-filled and traceable.
- Record the root cause and preventive action, not just the fix, so the same defect does not recur.

## qms
KEY: qms.intro_more

Pick a project, then work the tabs left to right. **ITP Plans** define the inspection and test plan with hold and witness points; activate a plan and open it to sequence hold points and link each control point to a BOQ position, CSI section, drawing or BIM element. **Inspections** record sign-offs against those points. **NCRs** capture defects: open an NCR to assign corrective actions, verify them, and close it out. **Punch List** tracks snags to close-out, and **Audits** cover internal, external and supplier ISO 9001 audits with ratings.

NCRs with a cost impact escalate against a real Variation Order (created first in Variations) and feed the Cost of Poor Quality rollup shown on the NCRs tab. You can export an audit-ready compliance dossier per ITP plan as CSV.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Link ITP control points to specs and BOQ positions so the compliance export proves coverage.
- Watch the Cost of Poor Quality tile to see what rework is really costing.

## inspections
KEY: inspections.intro_more

Press **New Inspection**, choose a type (structural, electrical, plumbing, fire safety, concrete, waterproofing or general), set the planned date, inspector and location, and build a checklist of items to verify, flagging the critical ones as hold points. The inspection starts Scheduled; press **Start Inspection** to move it to In Progress, then **Record Result** and choose Pass, Partial or Fail.

A fail or partial result unlocks one-click follow-up: **Create Punchlist Item** for a minor defect, or **Create NCR** to raise a formal non-conformance pre-filled from the inspection. Punch items flow to the Punch List, NCRs to the NCR register, and the whole loop traces back to the QMS overview. Export the full inspection log to Excel at any time.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Add a checklist with critical hold points so failures pre-fill the punch item or NCR you raise.
- Use Punch List for minor defects and NCR for formal non-conformances that need root-cause analysis.

## governance
KEY: governance.intro_more

Governance is one admin home with three tabs, and the URL remembers which tab you are on so a link reopens it. **Permissions** is a role-by-permission matrix where you set which role can do what across the platform. **Approval Routes** defines who signs off on records such as RFIs, submittals and change requests, in the order you choose. **Validation Rules** picks which standards a project is checked against, such as DIN 276, NRM, GAEB and BOQ quality.

What you set here drives the rest of the app: the permission matrix decides what users can reach, approval routes power the multi-step sign-off badges you see on submittals and other records, and the chosen rule sets are what Validation enforces. Roles tie back to User Management, and every change is recorded in the Audit Log.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Set approval routes before issuing records so sign-off runs as a tracked chain, not ad hoc.
- Match each project's validation rules to its region and standard so checks are relevant.

## collaboration
KEY: collaboration.intro_more

Open the hub for your active project and three live panels appear. **Project discussion** is a threaded comment feed you post, reply, edit and delete in; the same thread also shows in context on BOQ positions, Documents, RFIs and BIM elements. **Active now** is a live presence roster of who is connected to the project right now, plus a list of what you personally have open for editing. **Viewpoints** are saved markers for a discussion topic; add one with a title and description here, or save camera-anchored viewpoints from the BIM viewer and PDF takeoff.

Set your display name once and it labels your cursor and presence to teammates. Live co-editing rides on peer-to-peer WebRTC, which for persistent server-side sync needs a WebSocket provider configured in production.

**Getting the most out of it:**
- Anchor a discussion to a spot in the model by saving a viewpoint from the BIM viewer.
- Set your display name so teammates recognize your cursor when editing a BOQ together.
